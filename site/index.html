Robots that learn to move by themselves.

Mojito is a project to get a quadruped with 12 motors to learn a task from a simulation first, then refine it from real interaction. The robot does not exist yet, so this site shows the simulation, the maths and the measurements so far, and lets you change the robot and watch what happens.

Everything here is a simulation of a robot that does not exist. Dimensions, joint limits, masses and servo figures are placeholders; the servo figures are unverified retailer numbers. Stages 0 and 1 are pure geometry, so they say nothing about balance, torque or grip. Stage 2 adds physics, with placeholder everything and one run per case.

The robot

A quadruped with 12 degrees of freedom, three per leg (abduction and hip pitch at the shoulder, and a knee), with grippy pads for feet. Actuators are 20 kg·cm hobby servos on a 5–6 V supply; an ESP32 will drive them through a PCA9685 over I²C, with joint-angle sensors on each joint and learning done off board. None of it is built.

Why start in simulation

No robot exists yet, and the dimensions are still to be chosen. So the first job of the simulator is a design-exploration tool: given a set of dimensions, does the robot stand, does it walk, and are the servos inside their limits? Learning to walk comes after the model is trusted. The long-term loop is simulation for a first idea of the task, the real robot to refine it, and return trips to simulation to prepare for specific tasks.
